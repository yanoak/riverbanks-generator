# Script: Visa for a Hilsa (v2 draft, 2026-10-02)

*Approved by Yan on 2026-10-02. It replaces the v1 script tab once written into Edited Drafts.
The Monsoon Hilsa coinages it uses are now canon (Yan, 2026-10-02).*

## Why this revision: Sam's feedback (2026-10-02, relayed by Yan)

**The principle: readers learn the world as they read.** Every comic in the exhibition should
carry the ideas of the Riverbanks world, not just its characters.

**1. Shapla's reluctance can't arrive only at the end.** In v1, Shapla is a cook who brings the
feast and serves up the research fish by mistake. Then, on the last page, she suddenly delivers
her family's history of leaving in one speech. That's too late for it to carry weight: the
reader has no reason to feel it. Sam asked for:

- **A prologue page centred on forced migration and displacement.** It shows vignettes of
  several generations of Shapla's women, drawn as variations on her look, living through:
  - the Partition of India;
  - Bangladesh's war of independence;
  - the Ganges delta made uninhabitable by sea-level rise;
  - and at last, settling in the little village on the Meghna.
- **The point is to anchor Shapla as a character.** Then, when Jalal wants to follow the hilsa,
  her resistance is something the reader has already lived through with her.
- **Animals displaced alongside the people.** One panel shows creatures driven from place to
  place: by forest fire, by dams, by oil spills. Displacement isn't only a human story, and that
  panel leads straight into the hilsa's point of view on the next page.

**2. The ideas from the original draft shouldn't be lost.** Marcus and Shakil's original draft
(the *Monsoon Hilsa* tab) was full of ideas about how the Pluriverse would govern a migratory
fish, including:

- migratory stewardship and the Passage Covenant;
- the cybernetic, tagged hilsa;
- what happens when the monsoon moves and the fish follow it somewhere the rules don't expect;
- the gap between ecological time and human time;
- the Bengal Passage Cooperative's roots;
- borders, Partition, and people following the hilsa.

The Edited Drafts version deliberately made the story character-driven, centred on Jalal as the
main character. That was the right call, but it shouldn't cost the ideas the story exists to
carry. There is room to explore some of them across the arc of the comic, especially in **caption
boxes on large panels**, where they can teach without slowing Jalal's story down.

**How this draft answers it:**

- **Page 1, the new prologue:** Shapla's foremothers and the displaced animals. The brass pot ties
  the generations together and stays visible in every later scene, so her reluctance builds
  quietly from page 4 on.
- **Page 2, the hilsa's POV:** explains the Passage Covenant, the NeurOtolith and the nursery
  restoration in captions.
- **Page 3:** "custodial origin unresolved" becomes the real reason the fish is worth 0 taka.
- **Page 4:** brings in the moved monsoon, "one season is weather, three seasons is migration",
  and the channel unused for sixty years, which Jalal's metis explains.
- **Page 6:** turns border-free passage and the "schools" of people into Ya's offer.

## Canon note

Yan made the Monsoon Hilsa coinages canon on 2026-10-02:

- the Passage Covenant;
- the NeurOtolith;
- "custodial origin unresolved";
- "one season is weather, three seasons is migration";
- the Bengal Passage Cooperative's origins in fishers' co-ops;
- migratory "schools".

The Monsoon Hilsa tab's "World River Bank" is still not canon. The draft doesn't name who created
the Covenant.

**Format:** seven A1 boards: the prologue, then one per Edited Drafts scene. Each board is a 4 × 4
grid (cells 0–15). Budget: 80–120 words a page.

**Header (every page):** ACT FIVE / A VISA FOR A HILSA, with the page title below.

**Art style:** the house ligne claire style, using the cast sheets for Jalal, Shapla, Ya at 33,
Taro as a cat, the drone and Jalal's boat.

**Telemetry overlays** (page 2) are drawn as thin teal monospace text on dark water. They are
part of the art, not balloons.

**Motif:** Shapla's family's **brass pot**. Every generation carries it. It sits on a shelf in
Jalal and Shapla's hut, and on the last page it is in the boat.

## Page 1: Leaving (prologue)

Five women in a row of vignettes. They are variations on Shapla's face (same eyes and brow), each
dressed for her era, and each carries the same brass pot.

**1.1 (cells 0–1).** 1947. A teenage girl on the roof of a packed train, the pot in her arms.
- CAPTION: 1947. Bengal is cut in two. Her great-great-grandmother crosses a new border.

**1.2 (cells 2–3).** 1971. A young mother wades through paddies at night with a baby and the pot.
Fires on the horizon.
- CAPTION: 1971. A new country is born in war. Her great-grandmother runs, then comes home.

**1.3 (cells 4–7), wide.** The 2040s. Salt-white, dead rice in the Ganges delta; the sea pushing
up the channels. A mother leads a girl of eight, Shapla, through the water. The pot is on the
mother's head.
- CAPTION: The 2040s. The sea walks up the delta and the rice dies in salt. Shapla is eight.

**1.4 (cells 8–11), wide.** Today. A small hut by the Meghna, Jalal's boat at the bank. Shapla,
32, sets the brass pot on a shelf.
- CAPTION: At last, a village on the Meghna. Shapla puts the pot on the shelf, and means to leave
  it there.

**1.5–1.8 (cells 12, 13, 14, 15).** Four small square panels, wordless except for one caption
across the bottom of the row.
- **1.5:** Deer and a fishing cat flee a forest fire.
- **1.6:** Hilsa pile up against the concrete face of a barrage.
- **1.7:** A river dolphin and egrets in an oil slick.
- **1.8:** A single hilsa in open water, a metal glint at its gills, swimming out of frame
  towards page 2.
- CAPTION (across 1.5–1.7): The river's creatures have been moving too: from fire, from dams, from
  oil.

*≈ 105 words*

## Page 2: The Feed

**2.1 (cells 0–3), wide.** A Bengal Passage Cooperative lab. Ya at a bank of monitors, Taro asleep
on the desk. One screen reads NEUROTOLITH LINK: ACTIVE · SUBJECT H-117.
- CAPTION: 2065. Ya, thirty-three, studies food ecosystems for the Bengal Passage Cooperative.

**2.2 (cells 4–11), large, the hilsa's POV.** Underwater in the Meghna, looking upstream. The
mangrove roots and reedbeds are thick with juvenile fish. On the bank, nets are furled and boats
pulled up. Overlays: NURSERY RESTORATION ZONE 8 · HARVEST ABSTENTION YEAR 19 · EXTRACTION
SUSPENDED: COMPLIANCE 97.8%.
- CAPTION: Under the Passage Covenant, no one owns a migratory fish. The river holds the hilsa in
  passage.
- CAPTION: Every place it passes gives something (nursery, sediment, fresh water) and earns a share
  of the catch in return.
- CAPTION: A tag in its ear-stone, the NeurOtolith, records what the fish lives through.

**2.3 (cells 12–13).** The shoal thickens into thousands of silver bodies, and the fish turns into
a narrow channel. Overlays: SCHOOL CONTACT 2,437… · ROUTE MATCH 31% · ARCHIVAL CORRIDOR: DORMANT
· LAST TRANSIT 62 YEARS AGO.

**2.4 (cell 14).** Ya leans in, lit green by the screen.
- YA: This makes no sense. Why swim up a dead channel?

**2.5 (cell 15).** From below, a net closes over the fish. The overlay breaks up: LINK: SURFACED.

*≈ 100 words*

## Page 3: The Second-Hand Drone

**3.1 (cells 0–3), wide.** A flying drone bursts out of the river, the hilsa in its net. Jalal on
his boat.
- JALAL: I bought you second-hand at a night market. And look: the biggest hilsa I've ever seen!

**3.2 (cells 4–5).**
- JALAL: How much will this fetch at the ghat?
- DRONE: Beep boop. 0 taka.

**3.3 (cells 6–7).** Jalal throws up his hands.
- JALAL (shout): Idiot drone! I knew you were too cheap to work.

**3.4 (cells 8–11), wide.** The drone projects the fish's tag readout in the air: a yellow
**CUSTODIAL ORIGIN UNRESOLVED**. Jalal squints at it.
- DRONE: This fish swam a channel the Covenant hasn't mapped. No market may sell it.
- DRONE: But to the Bengal Passage Cooperative, it is priceless.
- CAPTION: The Cooperative grew out of fishers' co-ops. Now it runs cold stores, storm insurance
  and flood relief, and follows the hilsa for the KAN network.
- JALAL: The Bengal *what*?

**3.5 (cells 12–13).** The drone's lights spin.
- DRONE: I was KAN's, once. They can still call me. Incoming transmission!

**3.6 (cells 14–15).** Ya's avatar pops up above the drone. Jalal gapes.
- YA: That's a research hilsa! I'm Ya, from the Cooperative. May I visit? I have an offer.

*≈ 115 words*

## Page 4: The Visit

**4.1 (cells 0–3), wide.** Ya walks along the paddies by the river, Taro trotting beside her,
towards Jalal's hut.
- CAPTION: Taro, the kitten from the sailabout, is a teenager now. Cats live longer these days.

**4.2 (cells 4–5).** Jalal at the door, beaming.
- JALAL: Welcome! My wife has cooked you a feast.

**4.3 (cells 6–7).** Shapla sets down fish, vegetables, curries and rice, and keeps her eyes on
the stranger. The brass pot is on the shelf behind her.
- SHAPLA (thought): An "opportunity". Opportunities always mean packing.

**4.4 (cells 8–11), wide.** The meal. Ya shows a map on her tablet: a bright route up a channel
that most maps leave grey.
- CAPTION: This year the monsoon moved. The rain fell further north, and the hilsa followed it into
  a channel unused for sixty years.
- YA: The rule is one season is weather, three seasons is migration. Until then, those fish are
  "unresolved".

**4.5 (cells 12–15), wide.** Jalal taps the grey channel with a fishbone, matter-of-fact. Ya
stares at him.
- JALAL: They're not lost. My grandfather fished that channel before it silted up. The rains came
  from the north, so they've gone home.
- YA (thought): Months of data, and he just *knows*.

*≈ 115 words*

## Page 5: Where's the Fish?

**5.1 (cells 0–3), wide.** Ya, eager, sets down her spoon.
- YA: May I look at the fish?

**5.2 (cells 4–7).** Jalal, turning towards the kitchen.
- JALAL: My dear, could you bring the fish?

**5.3 (cells 8–11).** Shapla, puzzled, points at the table.
- SHAPLA: The fish is already on the table.

**5.4 (cells 12–14), wide.** Everyone stares at the dish of fish curry. Silence.

**5.5 (cell 15).** Under the table, Taro licks her lips.

*≈ 25 words. This is the comic beat, a breather between two dense pages.*

---

## Page 6: The Offer

**6.1 (cells 0–3), wide.** After an awkward silence, Ya speaks very politely.
- YA: Well, that's too bad. But my offer stands. Join us as a rivers and fisheries researcher.

**6.2 (cells 4–11), large, what Ya describes.** A map-like panorama of the hilsa's passage: from
the Bay of Bengal up the Padma and the Meghna, across borders drawn as faint dotted lines, with
small boats of fisher-researchers following the shoal.
- CAPTION: The hilsa has never needed a visa. Its passage runs through three countries' waters,
  and none of their borders.
- CAPTION: Under the Covenant, those who follow it for science travel on a passage too.
- CAPTION: KAN calls them schools: people who move with the fish and teach stewardship as they go.
- YA: Our researchers go wherever the fish go. The hilsa carry passage across every border, and so
  do the people who follow them.

**6.3 (cells 12–13).** Jalal, eyes shining.
- JALAL: Wherever the fish go…

**6.4 (cells 14–15).** Shapla, one hand on the brass pot, her face closed. (no text)

*≈ 110 words*

---

## Page 7: Wherever the Hilsa Goes

**7.1 (cells 0–3), wide.** Ya and Taro walk away along the paddies at dusk.

**7.2 (cells 4–5).** Jalal turns on his wife.
- JALAL: Look what you've done! That was our one chance to leave this village life.

**7.3 (cells 6–7).** Shapla holds her ground. Faint behind her: the four women of the prologue.
- SHAPLA: Leave for where? My family has done nothing but leave. Bengal. The delta. Now you want me
  to pack again?

**7.4 (cells 8–11), wide.** Jalal sits down beside her and considers this.
- JALAL: Not because life got too hard. This time we choose it. We follow the hilsa, and no border,
  no drought, no flood can turn us out.

**7.5 (cells 12–15), wide, final panel, wordless.** Jalal and Shapla's boat follows a silver shoal
downriver, with the drone overhead. Shapla glances back at the village. The brass pot is in her
lap.

*≈ 75 words*

## Inventions to confirm

1. **Promoting Monsoon Hilsa ideas to canon:** the Passage Covenant, the NeurOtolith tag, nursery
   zones and harvest abstention, "custodial origin unresolved", "one season is weather, three
   seasons is migration", the Cooperative's fisher-co-op origins, and the "schools" of
   people (from Marcus and Shakil's "global schooling"). The draft doesn't say who created the
   Covenant: the Monsoon draft says the "World River Bank", which isn't canon.
2. **Why the fish is worth 0 taka.** It used to be a joke about a dumb drone. Now it is the real
   rule: an unresolved fish can't enter the clearing system. The drone is still right that it is
   priceless to the Cooperative.
3. **The dormant corridor's "62 years"** puts its last transit in 2003, in Jalal's grandfather's
   working life. That fits "my grandfather fished that channel".
4. **The prologue's dates and generations.** 1947, 1971 and the 2040s are mine, as is Shapla
   being eight when the family leaves the delta. Edited Drafts says "great-great-grandparents
   from Bengal".
5. **The brass pot motif.**
6. **The animal vignettes.** The barrage and the oil spill echo real Ganges/Sundarbans history
   (Farakka; the 2014 Shela River spill), but no real names appear on the page.
7. **Shorter lines for Shapla and Jalal on page 7.** The prologue now carries her family history,
   so these are cut-down versions of the Edited Drafts lines.
8. **Left out of Monsoon Hilsa:** the upstream/downstream custody dispute, the Cooperative's
   "emergent passage zone" (its quiet capture of the fishers), and the Dhaka chef. Each needs its
   own scenes; they could seed another story.
