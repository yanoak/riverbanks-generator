## Why this revision

Sam's feedback (2 Oct 2026, relayed by Yan): every comic should carry the ideas of the Riverbanks world, not just its characters. Sam asked for three things, and this is where each one lands.

1. Shapla's reluctance can't arrive only at the end. A new two-page prologue follows her foremothers through Partition (1947), the war of independence (1971) and the drowned delta (the 2040s), to the village on the Meghna (page 1), then her own girlhood there (page 2). A brass pot travels with every generation and stands behind Shapla in every later scene, so her "My family has done nothing but leave" on page 8 is something the reader has already lived through.
2. Displacement isn't only a human story. The prologue ends on animals driven out by fire, dams and oil (2.5–2.7), and on one tagged hilsa (2.8) that leads into the hilsa's point of view on page 3.
3. The ideas of Marcus and Shakil's Monsoon Hilsa draft come back, in captions on the large panels: the Passage Covenant and the NeurOtolith (3.2); "custodial origin unresolved" as the real reason the fish is worth 0 taka (4.4); the Cooperative's roots in fishers' co-ops (4.4); the moved monsoon and "one season is weather, three seasons is migration" (5.4); border-free passage and migratory "schools" (7.2). Jalal stays the centre: his metis still solves Ya's puzzle (5.5).

## Khai's notes (3 Oct 2026), and where they land

1. A page between the prologue and the hilsa, on how Shapla came to the Meghna and met Jalal. As teenagers, Shapla's mother turns down PREMISE's funds: even the new system has a last mile, and generations of being moved on have taught the family not to trust anything from outsiders. This is the new page 2.
2. Jalal and Shapla have both had mercury poisoning since childhood, from a diet heavy in river fish. It reaches them only through the fish: pollution puts it in the river, the fish build it up, and people who eat the same river's fish every day build it up in turn. Planted on page 2 (2.4), found by Ya on page 8 (8.3), answered by a varied diet (8.4).
3. Ya's intervention changes. She doesn't win Jalal with a way out of poverty; she wins Shapla by cooking with her, not as another outsider with papers to sign, but as someone who treats her like family. Page 7 sets up the failure of the paperwork offer. On page 8 (Yan, 3 Oct) Ya doesn't leave: she stays the night, wakes to the couple arguing, and offers to cook breakfast with Shapla. Shapla warms to her and accepts Jalal joining KAN's data collection, and Ya tells her that following the fish also means a more varied diet.

4. They are not poor: nobody is, in the Pluriverse (Khai, via Yan, 3 Oct). The reason to move is a better diet. So no line frames the offer as a way out of poverty: Jalal wants to follow the hilsa, and Ya's case to Shapla is the varied diet that answers the mercury (8.4).

## Lettering and prompt rules (from the playbook)

No words in the art. Telemetry, the screens and the drone's readout are drawn as blank bars, dots and lines; every word and number on them is lettered as a label box in the house caption style.
Prompts describe only the scene. Room for lettering comes from named scenery (a broad pale sky, the dark open water above the shoal, a high bamboo-and-tin ceiling), never from "leave space".
Don't name frames, borders or edges in a prompt, even to forbid them. The hilsa in 1.8 swims "off to the right", not "out of frame".
Ya's avatar (4.6) is a figure in flat pale teal outlined in thin teal ink. Don't write "glow": it is on the style's avoid list.
On two-cell panels the faces fill the frame. Their balloons go across the border into the clear sky or ceiling of the panel above.
