## Why this revision

Sam's feedback (2 Oct 2026, relayed by Yan): every comic should carry the ideas of the Riverbanks world, not just its characters. Sam asked for three things, and this is where each one lands.

1. Shapla's reluctance can't arrive only at the end. A new prologue (page 1) follows her foremothers through Partition (1947), the war of independence (1971) and the drowned delta (the 2040s), to the village on the Meghna. A brass pot travels with every generation and stands behind Shapla in every later scene, so her "Leave for where?" on page 7 is something the reader has already lived through.
2. Displacement isn't only a human story. The prologue ends on animals driven out by fire, dams and oil (1.5–1.7), and on one tagged hilsa (1.8) that leads into the hilsa's point of view on page 2.
3. The ideas of Marcus and Shakil's Monsoon Hilsa draft come back, in captions on the large panels: the Passage Covenant and the NeurOtolith (2.2); "custodial origin unresolved" as the real reason the fish is worth 0 taka (3.4); the Cooperative's roots in fishers' co-ops (3.4); the moved monsoon and "one season is weather, three seasons is migration" (4.4); border-free passage and migratory "schools" (6.2). Jalal stays the centre: his metis still solves Ya's puzzle (4.5).

## Cast and places

Each cast member's look is pinned in the house style's cast. Name them in every panel prompt and spell out the details below again.
Jalal, 34: wiry, dark brown skin, ALWAYS a thick black moustache and no beard, short black hair; a white sleeveless vest, a blue-and-teal checked lungi, and an ochre gamcha over one shoulder. Barefoot.
Shapla, 32: dark brown skin, black hair in a low bun, a plain mangrove-green cotton sari, a steady, tired gaze. Small gold studs in her ears; no other colour on her.
Ya at 33: short black bob with a straight fringe, round wire glasses, ALWAYS the brick-red field jacket with chest pockets over a brown shirt. She carries her e-paper notebook from The Year It Snowed, the napkin pyramid still tucked into its cover.
Taro the cat: Ya's grey tabby, now a lean teenage cat. In a corner of a panel on every page from page 2 on. Nobody remarks on her.
The drone: battered, olive green with silt-brown patches, one round camera eye, a net slung beneath. Its lights are drawn as small coloured dots, never as a glow.
Shapla's foremothers (page 1 only, and as faint figures in 7.2): variations on Shapla's face, with the same eyes and level brows, each dressed for her era:
1947, her great-great-grandmother, about 15: a white cotton sari with a thin red border, hair in a long plait.
1971, her great-grandmother, about 20: a faded blue cotton sari, hair tied back, a baby in a sling.
The 2040s, her mother, about 35: a mangrove-green sari like Shapla's, a cloth bundle; she leads Shapla at eight, a thin girl with two short plaits in a cream frock.
Places and props, to prepare before building (playbook step 2):
Jalal's boat (in the cast already).
The brass pot (new prop, to create): a round-bellied brass water pot (kolshi) with a narrow neck and a flared lip, a dent on one side, worn bright where hands have carried it. Drawn in ochre.
Jalal and Shapla's hut (new place, to create): a small hut on a low earth plinth by the Meghna, woven bamboo walls and a corrugated tin roof with two small solar panels, a doorway onto a beaten-earth yard, banana plants and a jackfruit tree, paddies beside it and the river beyond, with Jalal's boat drawn up at the bank. Inside: a reed mat on a swept earth floor, a low wooden shelf on the back wall where the brass pot stands, a clay stove, a hurricane-style solar lamp. It appears on pages 1, 4, 5, 6 and 7.
The Cooperative's field station (2.1 and 2.4 only, described in the prompt): a timber station on stilts over the Meghna at night, a solar roof, a bank of screens.

## Through-lines

The brass pot: carried in 1947, 1971 and the 2040s (1.1–1.3), set on the shelf (1.4), behind Shapla at the feast (4.3, 5.3), under her hand during the offer (6.4), and in her lap on the boat (7.4).
The tag: a small ochre glint at the hilsa's gills (1.8, 2.2), the readout over Jalal's boat (3.4), the fish in the curry (5.4).
Two camera positions on the hut: from the paddy path outside (1.4, 4.1, 7.1), and from inside, across the mat towards the shelf (4.3, 5.4, 6.4).
Ya's notebook: the map of the dormant channel (4.4); Jalal taps it (4.5). It is the same e-paper folio Ya started in The Year It Snowed.
The river's direction: the hilsa swims upstream, out of the page's right edge (1.8, 2.2); the boat follows the shoal downriver at the end (7.4).

## Lettering and prompt rules (from the playbook)

No words in the art. Telemetry, the screens and the drone's readout are drawn as blank bars, dots and lines; every word and number on them is lettered as a label box in the house caption style.
Prompts describe only the scene. Room for lettering comes from named scenery (a broad pale sky, the dark open water above the shoal, a high bamboo-and-tin ceiling), never from "leave space".
Don't name frames, borders or edges in a prompt, even to forbid them. The hilsa in 1.8 swims "off to the right", not "out of frame".
Ya's avatar (3.6) is a figure in flat pale teal outlined in thin teal ink. Don't write "glow": it is on the style's avoid list.
On two-cell panels the faces fill the frame. Their balloons go across the border into the clear sky or ceiling of the panel above.

### Panel 1.1 (cells 0–1)

1947. A crowded train crosses flat Bengal country under a broad pale sky. On its roof, among refugees and bundles, a teenage girl in a white sari with a red border hugs the brass pot.

### Panel 1.2 (cells 2–3)

1971. Night. A young woman in a faded blue sari wades through a flooded paddy with a baby in a sling and the brass pot on her hip. Fires on the far horizon under a dark night sky.

### Panel 1.3 (cells 4–7), wide strip

The 2040s. Salt-white, dead rice fields in the Ganges delta, the sea pushing up the channels; abandoned huts stand in the water. A woman in a green sari leads Shapla, eight, by the hand through knee-deep water, the brass pot on her head. A broad grey-white sky.
! CAPTION: The 2040s. The sea walks up the delta and the rice dies in salt. Shapla is eight.

### Panel 1.4 (cells 8–11), wide strip

Today. Jalal and Shapla's hut by the Meghna, seen from the paddy path, Jalal's boat drawn up at the bank under a broad pale sky. Through the doorway, Shapla, 32, sets the brass pot on the shelf.
! CAPTION: 2065. At last, a village on the Meghna. Shapla sets the pot on the shelf, and means to leave it there.

! CAPTION (across 1.5–1.7): The river's creatures have been moving too: from fire, from dams, from oil.

## Inventions (approved by Yan, 2 Oct 2026)

These are not in Edited Drafts.
The Monsoon Hilsa coinages, now canon: the Passage Covenant, the NeurOtolith, nursery zones and harvest abstention, "custodial origin unresolved", "one season is weather, three seasons is migration", the Cooperative's fisher-co-op roots, and migratory "schools". The script doesn't name who created the Covenant (Monsoon Hilsa's "World River Bank" is not canon).
Why the fish is worth 0 taka: an unresolved fish can't be sold. The drone is still right that it is priceless to the Cooperative.
The dormant corridor's "62 years" puts its last transit in 2003, in Jalal's grandfather's working life.
The prologue's dates and generations (1947, 1971, the 2040s), and Shapla being eight when her family leaves the delta. Edited Drafts says "great-great-grandparents from Bengal".
The brass pot, and the animal vignettes. The barrage and the oil spill echo real Ganges and Sundarbans history, but no real names appear on the page.
Shorter lines for Shapla and Jalal on page 7, since the prologue now carries her family's history.
Added in the 3 Oct polish: the hut and the brass pot as cast members; Ya's notebook in place of the tablet; Taro on every page from page 2; the telemetry lettered as labels rather than drawn; page 5's punchline as one large silent panel; page 7's Ya leaving and Jalal's outburst merged into one panel, so the final boat gets half the board.
Left out of Monsoon Hilsa: the upstream/downstream custody dispute, the Cooperative's "emergent passage zone" and the Dhaka chef. Each needs its own scenes.
