**Slide 8: Pluriverse 101.**

*One purpose (of life):* **to live & let flourish.**

*Two Pluriverse planetary systems:*

- **PLURIVERS Trust** (Planetary League of United Rivers): following the ratification and
  respective opting-intos of the Pluriversal Covenant, the PLURIVERS Trust is the stewardship body.
- **KAN** (Kinetic Adaptation Network). *(The slide currently repeats PESA's description;
  Sam will fix it later.)*

*Three key Pluriverse institutions:*

- **PREMISE** (Planetary Reinsurance & Mitigation Services): the steward and underwriter of
  planetary risk. PREMISE was devised after the late-2027 collapse of the last global reinsurance
  company, as a reinsurer of last resort with authority over the loci and layers of planetary
  risk: the planet's waterways, airways and atmosphere.
- **PESA** (Planetary Ecosystem Services Authority): the "customer-support" authority responsible
  for ensuring that all beings and communities who depend on the planet can be supported as best
  possible.
- **PROSPER**. *(The slide currently repeats PESA's name and description; Sam will fix it later.
  Slide 7 has the description.)*

*The Four Teal Truths, the starting principles of the Pluriverse:*

1. **Ecology is wealth.** Wealth in the Pluriverse is not just an abstract measure, but a living
   index of life, and how abundant it is. Those who seek wealth learn to invest in ecology and how
   to help it grow richer.
2. **Circulation is health.** As with bodies and soils, healthy life comes from flowing
   circulations. Water and money foster life when they flow. To heal a system, unblock and
   facilitate its flows.
3. **Trust is capital.** What enables future ventures is not just abstract tokens or money, but
   what they represent: trust. In the Pluriverse, capitalism is really trustism.
4. **Free to agree.** Life and ecology flourish when beings are nourished at their own scales, but
   also able and empowered to come together and symbiote without coercion. A healthy ecology rests
   upon protecting this basic flexibility and freedom.

## World context (from the PLURIVERS world bible; background to the slides)

- **The logo ⨁, the Planetary Plus or Teal Cross.**
  - Teal is blue + green + brown, the three colours of rivers.
  - A cross in a circle means life and creation within planetary bounds.
  - PLURIVERS ~ RIVER PLUS.
- **PREMISE** pays *ahead* of need: communities facing foreseeable climate risk are pre-provided
  with funds or resources, including kinetic emergency and adaptation squads. Its motto: *"from
  each according to our interdependency, to each ahead of their need."*
- **PROSPER stakes ("Pucks")** are indexed to planetary ecological richness. They work like a
  nature index fund: borrowable, tradeable with safeguards against shorting, and invisible
  day to day. Local currencies carry on.
- **PESA** works like SaaS for the planet. Communities sign a Planetary Ecosystem Service
  Agreement (PSA) with service levels, conditions and recompense, and services get cheaper over
  time.
- **KAN** turns PREMISE's foresight into work. In the stories it runs **sailabouts**, experiential
  learning journeys along rivers inspired by the Aboriginal walkabout, with Young Leaders and
  Executive MBA programmes. It is also the network that research bodies such as the **Bengal
  Passage Cooperative** belong to.
- **The Gross Delish Index (GDI)** measures quality of life beyond money: flavour as a health
  report of the ecosystem behind the food. It has five tiers: Soil (base), Water, Seed, Air and
  Wind (tip). It comes from Jules's material, and the stories use it as canon: Dew lived it before
  it had a name.
- **The Great Confluence Accord (2030)** dissolves the failing top-down system and hands
  sovereignty to the land, the rivers and the people who tend them.

### Ismahan: The Youngest Delegate

*Trope: King Lear. An ageing Sultan weighs his succession between his two older sons and his youngest child, a daughter. The sons' confidence will come back to bite them.*


#### Cast

Sultan Ibrahim of Mua: ageing ruler, weary of foreign initiatives

The General (elder prince): commands the kingdom's military, and believes force and readiness are enough

The Financier (second prince): spent years on Wall Street, and believes his network can fund anything

Princess Ismahan: the youngest, just turned 18


#### Scene 1: The Invitation (late 2027)

The kingdom of Mua has barely survived another flood. An invitation arrives for the Sultan to attend a new Sunda Summit.

**SULTAN:** Another Western scheme. Another colonial project. It will be just like the SDGs. What is the point in going?

**THE GENERAL:** Father, the next time disaster strikes, my troops and I will deal with it. No problem. You don't have to rely on these foreign programmes.

**THE FINANCIER:** And you don't need their money. My friends and I can raise whatever the kingdom needs. Forget the summit. You don't have to go.

The Sultan turns to his youngest.

**SULTAN:** You know what? Let the girl go. She can learn how the world works, learn to sit with dignitaries, and represent our kingdom as its youngest delegate.


#### Scene 2: The Summit (July 2028)

Ismahan is 18 and feels out of place among the foreign dignitaries. She is happiest in the mangroves, talking with people in the villages and playing with the mudskippers.

But this summit is not like the ones her father and brothers complain about. It is built around people of the earth, the people who live closest to the rivers.

She finally lets her guard down.

Between sessions, she shares a meal with an older Thai woman from the Ping river valley delegation, who talks about food as if the whole river were inside it. Ismahan never catches her name.

**ISMAHAN (thinking):** This is something our kingdom can really rely on.


#### Scene 3: The Network Wakes (2028–2029)

Ismahan returns home, and the PLURIVERS network activates. The people who were already tending the rivers, the land and the ecosystems find themselves more and more empowered by the new mechanisms PLURIVERS sets in motion.


#### Scene 4: The Second Crisis (2029)

Floods and extreme weather combine into a food crisis. Once again the two princes rush to the throne room to save the day.

**THE GENERAL:** Don't worry, Father. We'll take care of it. My soldiers know exactly what to do, and they follow my every command.

**THE FINANCIER:** I'll call my best friends on Wall Street and the money will start flowing in.


#### Scene 5: Who Really Comes to the Rescue

The soldiers cannot cope with the sheer force of the destruction. Little by little they have to rely on the fishers, the villagers and the mountain people, who do the real work of rescue. The General still takes all the credit.

The Financier tries financial engineering and derivatives, but none of his usual lines of credit work. Eventually he turns to new PREMISE derivatives that have just become available. Even without him doing anything, they start paying out by themselves and flow to the people most in need.


#### Scene 6: The Throne Room (2030)

The brothers return, exasperated but holding it together, still bragging and taking credit. This time the Sultan is sceptical.

Ismahan enters, poised.

**ISMAHAN:** It isn't your two sons making this happen, Father. It's everyone who is part of this land and this kingdom, working together with its ecology.

The Sultan has to admit that the succession struggle no longer matters. The sultanate, the human and political institution, simply no longer holds so much power. The two princes are humbled.

**ISMAHAN:** In 1914, Johor had to accept a British General Adviser. It was inevitable, and there was nothing else they could do. More than a hundred years later we're in a similar predicament. But this time no foreign power is threatening us. We are walking into a new world, where humans take care of the land and the rivers, and the rivers take care of us.


#### Scene 7: The Confluence Accord (2030)

Sultan Ibrahim signs the Great Confluence Accord. It dissolves the failing top-down system and hands sovereignty to the land, the rivers and the people who tend them. The sultanate gives way to a pluriversal matriarchy: Ismahan, now 20, becomes the first leader of the new consensus-based kinship councils. Her brothers stand among the witnesses.

[End]


### Dew & Ya: The Year It Snowed

*Trope: Role-reversed mentorship. The young woman teaches the older one. The story is told through recollections: the present-day frame is 2052, and each lesson takes us back to Ya's childhood year with her grandmother.*


#### Cast

Ya: born 2032, 20 in the frame story. A middle-class girl from a Chinese immigrant family that settled on the Ping generations ago. She knows food, the land and the river deeply. Her sense of home is fluid: she treasures her immigrant identity and is open to taking on new ones. She is a restaurateur, not an academic, so she knows very little about the technical workings of the GDI.

Dew: Ya's grandmother (1964/65–2041). Runs the family restaurant in Chiang Mai, built on stilts on the Ping, and attended the 2028 Sunda Summit with the Ping river valley delegation. Her family's cooking heritage already lived by the principles of the Gross Delish Index long before it became the accepted way to measure quality of life beyond money.

Princess Ismahan: 42 in 2052, leader of Mua's kinship councils (from 'The Youngest Delegate'). She knows nature, rivers and ecology through a learned, academic upbringing, but she has never had to cook for herself from scratch. She can't cook, but she understands the GDI in technical detail. She also met Dew at the Sunda Summit and was inspired by her.

Taro, Ya's kitten: a tiny kitten Ya looks after on the boat. Nobody remarks on it; from time to time, in the panels, Ya feeds it little morsels.


#### Backstory: The Year It Snowed (2040)

Snow falls on Chiang Mai in winter for the first time in decades upon decades. It becomes the formative year of eight-year-old Ya's childhood. Over the course of it, Dew teaches her all the different ways of cooking that line up with what will become the GDI pyramid.

Right after that wonderful, memorable year, Dew passes away (2041). Ya dedicates her life to reconnecting with her grandmother's traditions and with the Ping River.


#### Frame: The Sailabout (2052)

The KAN network runs sailabouts, inspired by the Aboriginal walkabout. These are experiential learning journeys, something like today's fellowships, in which people connect with the rivers that form the bedrock of PLURIVERS. Ya, 20, is on a sailabout with KAN's Young Leaders programme, aboard a solarpunk boat on the Chao Phraya.

One day her new boatmate arrives: Princess Ismahan, 42, on KAN's Executive MBA programme during a sabbatical.


#### Main Arc: Bonding Through Food

A very young woman and an older one, from distinct backgrounds, bond over food. Ya teaches Ismahan the dishes she learned growing up in a Chinese restaurant in northern Thailand. In return, Ismahan explains the technical details of the GDI, which Ya has lived all her life without ever studying.


#### Act 1: The Base of the Pyramid

Ya is already aboard her sailboat on the Chao Phraya. Since morning she has been cooking her favourite meal, the Underfoot Taro Pot: a claypot of taro, mushrooms and dark greens, slow-braised until deep and earthy. Ismahan pulls up in a much fancier boat and climbs aboard as her new boatmate. Ya's first gesture is to offer her a bowl. It is the first meal they share, and it comes from the base of the pyramid: Soil.


#### Act 2: Connecting


#### Scene 1: The Failed Sesame Balls (Seed)

At a riverside village stop, they trade seeds with the locals to make Seed-Swap Sesame Balls, where every ball holds a different neighbour's seed. Ismahan has never cooked, and her attempt is a disaster: lopsided, burnt, falling apart. Ya laughs.

Then Ismahan explains the seed commons and how the Seed score is calculated. Ya realises that her grandma's habit of swapping seeds with the neighbours was the GDI before it had a name. It's the first swap of skills: Ya's hands for Ismahan's theory.


#### Scene 2: Mudskippers (Air)

At a mudflat stop, Ismahan spots mudskippers and lights up. As a girl in Mua she spent her days in the mangroves playing with them. They sit on the mudflat sharing fermented fruit, an Air food made by wild microbes nobody controls. Ya watches the princess she knows from the news turn back into that girl, and her guard comes down.

The talk turns personal. Ismahan talks about her brothers and how the kingdom changed; Ya talks about her immigrant family and her fluid sense of home. More and more, Ya says 'my grandma used to say…', which sets up the reveal at the shrine.


#### Act 3, Scene 1: Peaberry Rice in Chiang Mai

The sailabout ends in Chiang Mai, at the family restaurant on stilts on the Ping. Ya is thrilled to finally cook her grandmother's peaberry rice for Ismahan. While they cook, she tells the story of the year it snowed. Ismahan already feels connected to this grandmother she has only heard about.

**YA:** Oh, I can actually show you my grandma's shrine. The water seems to have gone down today.


#### Act 3, Scene 2: The Shrine

Dew's shrine can only be reached when the waters recede, so in a way she is at one with the river. The two women go down to it. The moment Ismahan sees Dew's digital avatar, she recognises her from the Sunda Summit.

Ismahan tells Ya what Dew taught her there when she was 18: food ties everything together. Food is not just about the humans eating it. It is about embracing the richness and biodiversity of everything it comes from. This was one of the lessons she carried home to Mua, and it inspired her to usher in the pluriversal matriarchy.

[End]


### Visa for a Hilsa


#### Cast

Jalal: a fisherman on the Meghna, and the main character. Where Ya's data confuses her, his intuition and metis let him diagnose the problem at once. In 2065 he decides to move from fishing to data collection.

Shapla: Jalal's wife. Her family's history is one of leaving: from Bengal, which is now part of a different country, then from the delta, which has gone under the sea, to this village.

Ya: 33, now a researcher studying food ecosystems (from 'Dew & Ya: The Year It Snowed'). Her sailabout with Ismahan inspired her to move from the restaurant into research. She has the data but not the intuition.

The drone: a second-hand fishing drone with a net, bought at a night market, with a chatty AI.

Taro, Ya's cat: the kitten from the sailabout, now in her teens. Cats live much longer now, and she is still very active.


#### Scene 1: The Feed (2065)

Ya, now in her thirties, sits at a monitor showing a live feed and a dashboard. We zoom into the feed: a single tracked hilsa in the Meghna River, Bangladesh.

Third-person view of the hilsa swimming upstream. It is spawning season, and tons upon tons of hilsa surround it. The signals on the dashboard are going haywire.

**YA:** Wait, this data makes no sense. I wonder what's happening to the hilsa in this river.

At the very end of the scene, the one hilsa she can see is scooped up by a net.


#### Scene 2: The Second-Hand Drone

A flying drone with a net attached bursts out of the river. Jalal sits on his boat, talking to it.

**JALAL:** I bought you second-hand at a night market. I didn't think you'd be any use. But look at you! You've caught the largest hilsa I've ever seen.

He hauls the hilsa onto the boat.

**JALAL:** Okay. How much do you think this one will fetch me at the market?

**DRONE:** Beep boop. 0 taka.

**JALAL:** Oh my god. Idiot drone, how can a fish this big be worth nothing! *sigh* Of course you don't work. I should have known you were too cheap to be any good.

**DRONE:** No, no. This one is actually very valuable. It is a research-tagged hilsa, tracked by the Bengal Passage Cooperative.

Jalal has no idea what the Cooperative is. He has never heard of it; he is just a simple fisherman.

**DRONE:** The Cooperative is a research organisation that is trying to help the fishers and farmers of this river system.

The drone explains the basic idea behind the tagged fish. It turns out the drone is second-hand because it once belonged to the KAN network, which the Cooperative is part of, so KAN researchers can still connect to it.

**DRONE:** Wait, wait! Incoming transmission, incoming transmission!

An avatar of Ya pops up.

**YA:** You've got a very important research hilsa. My name is Ya, from the Cooperative, and I'd like to come and visit your house to tell you about an interesting opportunity.

Jalal has never seen anything like this. But he is excited that someone from far away, who talked to him through a robot, is coming to his house. All he knows is that this fish is important.


#### Scene 3: The Visit

Ya walks through the village along the paddy fields by the river, her cat Taro at her side. The cat was a tiny kitten on the sailabout; cats live much longer now, so she is in her teens but still very active. They walk up to Jalal's little hut.

**JALAL:** Welcome, welcome! My wife has cooked you a feast. I can't wait to learn more about this opportunity.

Jalal's wife Shapla brings in a fantastic feast: fish, vegetables, curries and the best rice.

Over the meal, Ya explains how the Cooperative uses biomechanically tagged fish to explore the rivers, and that the fish Jalal caught is one of them, collecting important data about the river. Jalal says something right off the cuff that solves the puzzle Ya has been stuck on. She is immediately impressed by this humble fisherman.

**JALAL:** They're not lost. My grandfather fished that channel. The hilsa used to come up the old river before it silted up. The rains came from the north this year, so they've gone home.


#### Scene 4: Where's the Fish?

**YA:** May I look at the fish?

Jalal remembers he left it in a jar of water in the kitchen.

**JALAL:** My dear, could you bring the fish?

**SHAPLA:** Oh, the fish is already on the table.

She points at the dish of fish curry. Awkwardness ensues. Under the table, Taro licks her lips at the curry.


#### Scene 5: The Offer

After an awkward silence, Ya speaks very politely.

**YA:** Well, that's too bad. But my offer still stands. If you'd like to join the research network as a rivers and fisheries researcher, I'd be very happy to bring you into the recruitment. Our researchers go wherever the fish go. The hilsa carry passage across every border, and so do the people who follow them.


#### Scene 6: Wherever the Hilsa Goes

Ya says goodbye and leaves. Jalal turns on his wife.

**JALAL:** Look what you've done! You've ruined the one chance we had to leave this village life.
