# Epilogue rework: the seeds, not the flood

## Context

The current Epilogue, *Moving House* (comic `MH`, script
`content/drafts/2026-10-03_moving-house_v1.md`, plan `2026-10-03_moving-house-boards`), is built
on Khairun's flood story: a crisis forces the House of Taming to choose release over defence in
2090. Yan and Khai are reworking it so that it does **not** rest on another crisis. By 2075 the
ecoarchy has made crises far rarer, so the ending should show a choice made in calm, not under
pressure.

Worked out between Yan and Claude on 2026-10-04. Khai has given Yan free rein to take her ideas and
run with them. Not yet in Edited Drafts, so none of it is canon until it lands there.

## Goal

A v1 script for the new Epilogue that Yan and Khai can react to, in
`content/drafts/2026-10-04_epilogue-seeds_v1.md`. Its premise: the matriarchs of Muara dissolve the
Matriarchy and the idea of a people tied to a land. Ismahan teaches Teja why and how, and Teja
carries it out at Ismahan's funeral.

## The story so far

### Part I: Svalbard, 2075 (Teja 11, Ismahan 65)

- Ismahan takes Teja to the Svalbard seed vault. They bring seeds to swap with a Sámi elder, who now
  looks after the vault, and Ismahan teaches Teja history.
- **The bunker mentality.** In the age before the ecoarchy, crisis was everywhere, and safety meant
  piling things up and drawing lines round what was yours: banks, capitalism, borders. The vault is
  that mentality in its literal form, a bunker full of dormant seeds.
- **A phase, not an evil past.** Modernity was a phase that humanity had to steward itself through.
  Ismahan's metaphor is **the seed coat**: a hard shell that keeps the seed alive through winter, and
  must crack before anything can grow. Teja holds a seed and feels the shell.
- **Finance can change in a day; institutions take decades.**
  - A crisis built Bretton Woods (planned 1941–44; the conference ran three weeks in July 1944), and
    one day broke it (the Nixon shock, 15 August 1971).
  - PLURIVERS used a crisis, the six megadisasters of 2027, to reset 21st-century finance.
  - But borders, states and visas stayed. Ismahan led Muara as the first polity to embrace the new
    world. It took decades for everyone else to give up institutions that no longer made sense.
  - The Westphalian system breaks down when an island nation's land sinks; Tuvalu's 2023
    constitution already declares its statehood continues even if its land does not. It also fails
    Zomia, where hill peoples define themselves by altitude (valley, hill, mountain), not by extent
    on a map (James C. Scott).
- **Jalal and Shapla as the parable.** Isma passes on their story:
  > **ISMA:** Your mother's partner and my dear friend, Ya, once told me about this couple…

  Their choice to leave their village is a small version of what all of humanity has to do.
- **The new project.** After nearly five decades (2028–2075), the new world is mature and Ismahan's
  work is done. Her last project is to dismantle the Matriarchy and dissolve the idea of a people
  tied to a land. Her guiding line: ***"Stop coming to me. Go to each other."***
- **Why the elder is Sámi.** Svalbard has no Indigenous people; Sápmi is on the mainland. The script
  needs to give a reason, and give her a view of her own: *"You call it a phase. To us it was
  weather. We waited it out."*

### Part II: Ismahan's funeral, 2090 (Teja 26, Ismahan dies at 80)

- Teja gives the oration, as a granddaughter, not an heir: there is no Matriarchy left to inherit.
- She opens with Ismahan's words: *"Stop coming to me. Go to each other."*
- Her declaration is an **invitation, not a decree** (*free to agree*): each person chooses whether
  to take it up.
- **Muara goes from a proper noun back to a common one.** *Muara* means a river mouth, where fresh
  water meets salt. The people of Muara stop being a polity and become estuary people, defined by
  the water, like Zomia's altitude peoples. Draft lines:
  > From today, Muara is not who we are. It is what we are.
  > Wherever a river meets the sea, wherever two waters mix, that is muara. And whoever lives
  > there, we are their people.
- Soraya and Ya stand together, a couple for twenty years, without comment.

### What renunciation means in an ecoarchy

Giving wealth away is normal when *circulation is health*; hoarding is the vice. So the radical act
is giving up **being needed**. Muara does all three at once:

| Today | Ecoarchic equivalent |
|---|---|
| A billionaire giving everything away | A trusted steward telling people to go to each other instead (the mother tree) |
| A royal abdicating | Ending the line: no ninth generation; Teja is heir to nothing |
| The UN dissolving | Handing stewardship and the PROSPER stake to the river as a legal person (cf. Whanganui, 2017) |

Possible closing exchange:
> **TEJA:** Will PLURIVERS dissolve one day too?
> **ISMAHAN:** I hope so. That's how we'll know it worked.

## Decisions (Yan, 2026-10-04)

- The Epilogue no longer rests on a crisis.
- **Ya and Soraya are partners.** Ya is **not** Teja's parent. Soraya joins KAN in **2070**, when
  Teja is 6 (Soraya 31, Ya 38), and the two get together through KAN.
- Muara turning from a proper noun into a common noun is the closing idea.
- **Taro becomes a beaing** (2026-10-05): a biological cat until her late teens, then
  cybernetically enhanced into an embodied AI companion, after Seapunk's *Southbeast Asia*. Proposed
  year ~2070. She keeps all her biological memories. She speaks at the funeral; she could not
  before.
- **The Epilogue's lettering is fuller** (2026-10-05), in the register of the Prologue and Act One:
  long capitalised captions that teach the world and name their sources, about 200–280 words a
  board, rather than the playbook's 80–120 for the middle acts. The punchlines stay short.
- **The tunnel panel is cut** (2026-10-05): its meltwater irony pulled towards crisis and undercut
  Ismahan's point that the vault was a kindness.
